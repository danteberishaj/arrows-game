## Decisions

1. **A point target, not a band.** The table's `baseCells` and the tier texture give one target per (level, tier), so
   level 1 is exactly the owner's 88. The first RNG draw is still taken (`next(t, t + 1)` returns t), so the stream
   sits where W3-10 put it. Level-to-level variety comes from the shape bag, row rounding and the fill.
2. **The tier texture as cell ratios, with Normal = 1.** A tier's target is `baseCells × w / w.Normal`, so `baseCells`
   is the Normal target and row 0's 88 is level 1's. That works because level 1 is Normal.
3. **The ceiling in cells** is measured against 250 arrows at both the neutral bias and the tail floor (0.3), so all
   six candidates share one table value.
4. **The bias in the base candidates** falls linearly 3 → 1 on the cells' schedule. That keeps "differ only in the
   saturation level" true. The consequence: every level from 2 up to the ceiling carries a b between 1 and 3 that the
   owner has not seen. Every such value is flagged "look not accepted" (Concern 3).
5. **The bias tails** go 1 → 0.3 by level 3000 and are flagged "look not accepted":
   - 0.3 is the mildest value below 1 that W3-11 measured.
   - Its brand gate flags 0.3.
   - The brief conditions tails on "passed its brand checks", which no value below 1 did. They are kept as exploration
     only, as the controller's flag rule anticipates.
   - 2–4 candidates for the owner: the sheet shows S100, S400, S1000 and S400b. The doc has all six.
6. **Aspect is kept under the column cap** (`v2MaxRows`). W3-10's cols-only clamp squeezed wide shapes; for example a
   Rectangle at 46×46 had "capacity" 2116. With 37 columns that squeeze would have hit every wide shape.
7. **(b) "within one cycle's spread of the ceiling":**
   - Read as: the first 10-cycle (60-level) running median of cycle-median arrows that reaches the lower quartile of the
     candidate's own plateau (the cycles past its saturation row).
   - A single-cycle first-crossing was tried first. It is noise-driven: S1000 and S1000b gave 751 and 901 on identical
     boards.
8. **(a) is judged on 300-level blocks** against a flat-curve reference over 12,000 levels. 60-level blocks are printed,
   but a flat curve moves up to 34% between them.
9. **(g)** prints the brief's single cycle, plus the median over the 10 cycles a switching player meets next, because
   single cycles are noisy (v1's own cycle at 400 is 243 against a typical 185).
10. **The window model** (W3-10 decision 1, top-k windows) is confirmed: its cost is measured at 0–1 slots in 3000
    levels.
11. **`V2_CURVE` = S400 as the placeholder**, the grid's middle. It is not a recommendation.
12. **The window-target memo** in `curveWindowMaxTarget` was added because the cold pick at level 20000 was slower than
    BASE. It is output-identical.
