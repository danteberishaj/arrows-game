## Decisions

1. **The knob is a `generate` argument, not a module setting.** `generate(i, 2, { clearableBias })` stays pure.
   W3-14's curve rows can feed it per level, and the probe's `--bias` is the same call. A knob with v1 throws
   (tested).
2. **The biased path picks with `u * total`, not `trunc(u * total)`.** With fractional weights, truncation gives a
   candidate a chance of either zero or a whole unit, depending on where its interval falls. For integer weights
   the two picks select the same candidate (`floor(x) < n` iff `x < n`), so b = 1 reproduces the neutral corpus
   exactly (tested). M7 shows the difference is observable.
3. **Lane-empty heads are marked once, as a per-cell direction bitmask.** A first version used a per-candidate
   closure over the mask bounds. It measured about 7% slower at b = 1; see Timing. Both deal identical boards.
4. **Set (i)'s noise band.** v2 levels are fixed per index, so "b = unset over 5 seeds" is built by refilling each
   level's own v2 mask with `DotNetRandom(1..5)`. The mask, size and tier do not depend on b, and the probe
   asserts that. The level's real stream (`generate(i, 2, knobs)`) is shown beside it for reference.
5. **Aggregates.** Clearable@0, scan, blocked and arrows are medians over boards. Bend rate and mean length are
   pooled over all arrows, because they are properties of the arrow population. A b is compared against the band
   by the **median of its 5 replicas**, and the doc also marks whether the replica ranges overlap.
6. **The v2 neutral pin (`04d0ec7e`) is re-pinnable**, unlike v1's. A later task that changes v2 content on
   purpose re-pins it with its own evidence: W3-09's clamp, W3-14's curve, or W3-18's shapes. The test comment
   says so.
7. **Set (ii) uses v2's sizing formula** (`v2Cols`) and the Normal config. `--size-sweep` also accepts rows 6–7,
   which v2 never deals, for the red-team reproduction; the header says so.

## Concerns

1. **Brand gate: 0.1, 0.3 and 10 "change arrow look", and b = 10 shows a visible rim fringe.**
   - The numeric shifts are small: at most 0.10 cells of mean length and 2.7 points of bend rate.
   - But the b = 10 screenshot shows the outward-row fringe the brief worried about, and b = 3 (numerically clean)
     shows a milder one.
   - The numeric gate can pass a look the eye might reject. It measures bend and length, not head alignment. A
     head-alignment metric could close that gap (for example, the share of rim heads pointing outward, or runs of
     parallel adjacent heads). That would be a follow-up, not built here.
2. **The knob makes v2 generation about 1–3% slower when set** (node, JIT). It also re-rolls the heavy tail, up to
   283 arrows against 264 unset over 10,000 levels.
   - W3-10 inferred about 79 ms on device for its 264-arrow board, against W2-04's 85 ms swap backstop.
   - A 283-arrow board, plus the path cost, could cross it. INFERRED, UNVERIFIED-DEVICE: Hermes and iOS are
     unmeasured.
   - This only bites once a curve sets the bias (W3-14). Neutral is unchanged.
3. **The v2 neutral pin (`04d0ec7e`) is now a test.** W3-09, W3-14 and W3-18 will change v2 content on purpose,
   and must re-pin it with their own evidence (the test comment says so). This is by design (brief acceptance 1),
   but it is a new tripwire for those tasks.
4. **`fillMask` is no longer "byte-untouched"** (W3-10's phrasing). The brief places the knob there, and ruling
   W3-1 forbids a copied module. v1 and the daily stay pinned: 5 goldens, the v1 corpus and daily 0..364 are
   unchanged, and mutation M9 shows the pins catch a leak.
5. **The `DotNetRandom` defect (W3-10 Concern 1) is unchanged.**
   - The knob adds no RNG draw: b = 1 reproduces the neutral corpus.
   - Sets (i) and (ii) use in-range seeds 1..5 for their replicas.
   - The "real v2" stream uses `seed(i)`, as W3-10 did. About a quarter of those streams still leave [0, 1) from
     draw 3 on.
   - The biased pick handles out-of-range draws exactly like the neutral pick (u < 0 picks the first candidate,
