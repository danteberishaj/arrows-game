**(b) Rounded outline corners.** `maskOutlinePath(mask, cell, radiusCells = CLEAR_REVEAL_CORNER_RADIUS_CELLS)`:
- Every staircase corner (convex and concave, outer contours and holes) is replaced by a circular fillet: a cubic
  quarter circle with arm `4/3 (sqrt 2 - 1) r` (radial error at most 0.027 %).
- r = `min(0.5 cell, half of each adjacent run)`. Each run's two end corners take at most half of it each, so
  fillets can meet but never overlap. Every fillet stays inside the r x r square at its corner, inside a filled
  cell (convex) or an empty cell (concave), so different loops never meet.
- Two loops through one grid point (diagonal cells, e.g. the Bolt's tips) both turn right there (convex), so they
  pull apart by ~0.41 r instead of touching.
- Output: `M / L / C / Z`, each loop ending exactly at its start, coordinates printed to 3 decimals.
- `radiusCells <= 0` returns the old staircase string, byte for byte.
- Stroke width, colour, joins and the 2 pt minimum are unchanged.
- Measured on the 7 preview masks: rounded → staircase at most **0.146 cell**, staircase → rounded at most
  **0.207 cell**. These match the analytic 0.293 r and 0.414 r (`artifacts/W5-17-ROUND/deviation.txt`).

### Follow-up 2026-10-06 (2): smooth the contour, not the corners

The coordinator reviewed the preview and found that the `'corners'` scallops read as wobbly, which misses "reads as
a smooth shape". The new default is `CLEAR_REVEAL_OUTLINE_SMOOTHING = 'contour'` (`artConfig.ts`). `'corners'` stays
available as the fallback; `maskOutlinePath(mask, cell, 'none')` is the old staircase.

**Method** (`silhouette.ts` `cutCorners` + `roundedPolygonPath`):
1. Every staircase corner is cut back 0.5 cell along both of its runs (clamped to half of each run; all runs are at
   least 1 cell, so it is always 0.5). The cut points are the 1-cell runs' midpoints and are exact half-integers.
   Repeated points are dropped and straight-through points merged, so a run of 1-cell steps becomes one straight 45°
   line, and a long run keeps all but 0.5 cell at each end.
2. Every remaining polygon vertex (135° or 90°) is rounded with one quadratic curve. The control point is the vertex.
   The ends sit `min(0.5 cell, half of each adjacent side)` back along the sides, so neighbouring curves meet at
   most and the outline is tangent-continuous.
3. The output is `M / L / Q / Z`: each loop ends exactly at its start, coordinates to 3 decimals, holes included.
   The stroke is unchanged.

Why no self-intersection: each cut stays in the half-cell triangle at its corner, the same region as a 0.5-cell
fillet: a filled cell when convex, an empty one when concave. Corners of one cell that belong to different loops
are always diagonal. A diagonal pinch (two loops through one grid point) becomes two separate loops. The numeric
check covers the 6 degenerate fixtures, the pinch (the loop gap is > 0.25 cell) and every distinct v1 shape (26)
plus a non-square daily.
